<p class="lead">Wave-load problems span wave theory, structural hydrodynamics and random processes, and no single book covers all of them. The following are standard references in the field, grouped by purpose, each with a note on its scope.</p>

## Wave theory

**Dean & Dalrymple, *Water Wave Mechanics for Engineers and Scientists* (1991).** An engineering-oriented text on wave mechanics. Starting from the linearised boundary-value problem, it derives the dispersion relation, particle kinematics, wave energy and group velocity, and then treats nonlinear waves and wave forces on structures. The second-order Stokes wave and Stokes drift in the theory section of the [wave lab](/en/updates/wave-lab/) follow its derivations.

**Mei, Stiassnie & Yue, *Theory and Applications of Ocean Surface Waves* (2005).** Divided into linear and nonlinear parts and considerably more advanced than the previous book, it gives a complete mathematical treatment of long waves, diffraction and radiation, nonlinear shallow-water waves and modulational instability. It is suited to consultation when a systematic derivation is required.

## Wave loads on structures

**Faltinsen, *Sea Loads on Ships and Offshore Structures* (1990).** A standard reference on wave loads on offshore structures, with concise treatments of the Morison equation, diffraction theory, mean drift forces, slow-drift motion and slamming. Its physical explanations are clear and its length is moderate.

**Newman, *Marine Hydrodynamics* (1977).** A classic MIT textbook covering potential flow, added mass, slender-body theory and the motion of bodies in waves. It is particularly useful for understanding the physical origin of added mass and radiation damping.

**Sumer & Fredsøe, *Hydrodynamics Around Cylindrical Structures* (revised edition, 2006).** A treatment of the viscous flow around cylinders, including vortex shedding, the Keulegan–Carpenter number, and the drag and inertia coefficients of the Morison equation. It complements potential-flow methods, which do not account for viscous effects.

## Random waves and design

**Holthuijsen, *Waves in Oceanic and Coastal Waters* (2007).** A clear account of wave observation, wave-height statistics, wave spectra and directional spectra, which also covers the generation and propagation of waves in coastal waters. The irregular-wave part of the wave lab also draws on this book.

**Goda, *Random Seas and Design of Maritime Structures* (3rd edition, 2010).** Starting from the statistical properties of random waves, it treats the selection of design waves and the design of maritime structures, drawing on extensive engineering experience.

## Mathematical methods

**Linton & McIver, *Handbook of Mathematical Techniques for Wave/Structure Interactions* (2001).** A systematic collection of analytical methods for wave–structure interaction problems, including separation of variables and eigenfunction expansions, multipole expansions and integral equations. For diffraction and radiation by simple geometries such as cylinders and plates, the procedures in the book can be followed directly.
